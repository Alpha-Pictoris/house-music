# The B-side

*Playable discovery.*

## The rug’s shadow

Lay a **Woven rug** in a room with a **Lamp**. A flat rug should not cast a shadow, but this one does. It lies a little way out from under the rug’s front edge, and every few seconds it stretches further and draws back. A rug in a room without a lamp has none. With reduced motion the shadow rests in place.

Tap the shadow and the house turns over like a record. The turn button beside undo does the same.

## The other side

The rooms keep the shapes you drew. The walls, the plain furniture and the musicians are gone. Each musical furnishing is a shadow standing where the piece stands, and the underside of the rug shows where you came through. Tap it to turn the house back. Choosing **Build** or **Furnish** also turns it back.

Drag a shadow and it plays from its new spot on this side only. From then on the two sides take turns. One pass plays the furniture where it stands and the next plays the shadows where you left them, so a house with one furnished room can have a verse and a chorus. With **House band** on, a shadow sits on the nearest half beat. Objects on a shelf play on the A-side only.

A shadow stays where you left it when its furnishing moves about the room. Drop it where the piece stands, or choose **Call shadow back**, and it follows the piece again. Carrying the piece to another room, copying it or making it plain does the same. A shadow dropped outside its room keeps its last place.

From the keyboard, the bracket keys step through the shadows and the arrow keys move the selected one.

## The monitor

An old monitor stands on a clear patch of floor on the B-side, with a green dot on its glass. Double-click or double-tap the glass, or press Enter. It shows **EH/98/041** and a link to the Portsmouth City Record Office. The link carries the sounding house, so the cassette in that file plays the tune you made, both sides in turn. [The cassette archive](cassette-archive.md)

When no shadow starts in the last two beats of the B-side pass, three falling tones answer there in the key of the record, and the dot flares with them.
