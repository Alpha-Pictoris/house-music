# The cassette archive

The Portsmouth City Record Office is a fictional municipal archive for House Music. The public catalogue sets the Rigger’s Yard files among historical entries for Portsmouth, including Conan Doyle’s Southsea correspondence, the Portsea tithe map and the Gosport ferry ticket office. Each historical entry links to its original catalogue or heritage record. The Rigger’s Yard residents, files, dates and references belong to the story.

## The complaint

In file **EH/98/041**, the resident in the lower flat sends a recording of music heard beside the meter cupboard. Open the file with a shared house in its address and the tape plays that house’s tune. Visitors who arrive by browsing hear the office’s reference copy.

The resident asks for the tape with the blue label. The deposit register records two labels, one over the other. Lifting the white label on the cassette sleeve uncovers **HO/78/017**, a housing inventory dated twenty years earlier. The shelf mark also works in the catalogue search.

The earlier file holds a stairwell test strip. Opening it in House Music reveals two landings. Both play the same descending figure. The bass answers half a beat later on the lower landing. The player can rearrange the house and keep their own copy. [Playback and local preview](cassette-player-plan.md)

## Later chapters

The proposed entrance from the house is a defunct CRT on the B-side. Leave a gap at the end of a phrase and three notes answer in the same key. On the last note, a green dot fades from the glass. Opening the monitor would send the sounding house to the complaint file. [The B-side](b-side.md)

A possible later chapter would put a telephone number on the sleeve. A text containing the case number could reach the resident through Twilio. The paper trail already leads to the older file.
