# Designing the house

## A playable house

The first gesture draws a room on bare ground. It belongs to the player before a single note sounds. Drawing out from inside a room adds an alcove or a wing. Where two rooms share part of a wall, a doorway can join them along that span. A cancelled drag keeps the last placement. One undo restores both a removed room and the furniture it held.

The floor is the score. With the house band off, one unit along the sweep is one beat. A wardrobe and a mirror at the same position along the sweep can start together even on opposite sides of the room. Move one a unit farther along and it enters a beat later. During playback, a placement takes effect on the next pass so the sweep and the phrase change together.

The cutaway uses coloured ink over dark ground. Furniture outlines have a slight wobble at 0.8 CSS pixels. The drawing changes about nine times a second while the camera and sweep move smoothly. A player can still grab the same corner of a piece as the line around it shifts.

## Rooms, fittings and the garden

Someone furnishing a bedroom should be able to choose a bed for its shape and still give it the part they want to hear. Fittings make the instrument visible. Iron rings carry the bass, brass studs the drums, and plain pieces stay silent. The arrangement supplies the recordings. A thorn carving can play a filtered clavinet in **Chrome Attic** and a low guitar in **Moth Mass**.

The house band fits a handful of active pieces to a shared groove. A one-bar house can play a different answer on its second pass. Three masked musicians sway through the rooms during playback and stand still when the player pauses.

Rugs fit under furniture, windows attach to outside walls and a path can lead up to the front door. Small objects belong on tables and shelves, where they can stay silent or carry a short accent. Even the finish matters to the sound. Moonstone tile gives a note a short echo, and moss carpet softens its high frequencies.

## Save, share and remix

A local save, a house file and a share link carry the same rooms, furnishings, fittings and arrangement. Switching records keeps the current beat. Saving the house preserves the chosen record, so a friend opening the link hears the phrase with the same instruments.

The recipient opens a separate draft. They can change the ending and choose **Save a copy** to give it a place in their library. Reopening the original link plays the layout the sender shared. A house file carries the same snapshot when a link grows unwieldy. The cassette in the record office uses that snapshot to play the sender’s tune on another site.
